            Executive summary

            —

            Structural observations

            • Energy/commodity-driven deficits (Gulf-MENA, CAR) — large and volatile, moving with global oil & gas prices more than bilateral policy.
            • Re-export hub effects (UAE inside Gulf-MENA, Singapore/Hong Kong inside ASEAN & East Asia) — headline country figures overstate the true bilateral relationship because goods are often in transit.
            • Diversified, mature corridors (Europe, USA-Canada) — the most balanced growth profiles and closest to parity or surplus.
            • Small, high-growth corridors (Oceania, BIMSTEC) — modest in dollar terms today, but compounding faster than several "core" corridors.

            Strategic considerations

            • Deficit concentration: three corridors carry essentially all of the portfolio's net deficit — highest-leverage target for diversification is East Asia, given its size and negative export trajectory.
            • Surplus durability: the USA-Canada surplus is the most durable — both flows grow at similar, moderate rates, unlike BIMSTEC's surplus, which is compressing as imports outgrow exports.
            • Single-country exposure: CAR (Russia), USA-Canada (USA) and Gulf-MENA (UAE) all carry concentration above ~68% in at least one flow direction — a shock to any one of those three countries would move the entire corridor.
            • Coverage gap: the residual outside these ten corridors is small (<4% of exports, <2% of imports) but non-zero — likely smaller West Asia and South Asia partners plus unclassified DGCIS categories.

            08 · Methodology

            How this was built, and where it can break.

            SOURCE

            DGCIS EIDB, corridor-wise

            Every figure originates from India's Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (tradestat.commerce.gov.in), downloaded corridor by corridor and cross-validated — overlapping years across separate downloads were checked to match exactly.

            SCOPE

            10 corridors, 6 fiscal years

            Gulf-MENA, BIMSTEC, CAR (Russia/CIS), ASEAN, LAC, Europe, USA-Canada, Africa, East Asia and Oceania — FY2020–21 through FY2025–26, all six complete and closed. CAGR is computed on the full 5-year span between FY2020–21 and FY2025–26, the latest available year.

            CAVEAT

            Regional labels, not political blocs

            Corridors follow DGCIS country groupings. Some entries — UAE (Gulf-MENA), Singapore and Hong Kong (ASEAN / East Asia) — are regional re-export hubs, so bilateral totals can overstate direct trade with the goods' economy of origin.
